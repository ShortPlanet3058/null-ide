Privacy

Null collects nothing. It has no telemetry, no analytics and no crash reporting. It will not transfer any information to other networked systems unless you ask it to.

What it contacts, and only when you ask

  • The AI provider you choose, when AI is on and you use it (it's off until you turn it on): the code and question you send go to that provider (Anthropic, OpenAI, Mistral, Groq, Google, OpenRouter, NVIDIA), or stay on your computer with Ollama. API keys are kept in your system's key store, never in a file.
  • Language servers, when you click Install: they're downloaded with the tool that ships them (npm, rustup, go, dotnet), or from their makers' releases on GitHub; when a server needs Node.js and the computer has none, Null downloads the official build from nodejs.org. Every download Null makes itself is checked against the checksum it was built with.
  • New versions, if you turn on “Tell me when a new version is out” (once a day) or choose Check for Updates: Null reads its latest release's details from GitHub. Nothing about you or your files is sent. Nothing is downloaded or installed until you click Update: the package is then downloaded from that release and checked against details signed with Null's own key before it's installed.
  • Servers you open (a folder over SSH, or inside WSL): through your own ssh, keys and settings, or Windows' own WSL. A password ssh asks for is kept in memory until Null quits, never written down.
  • Git and GitHub, when you fetch, pull or push, or open a pull request: through your own git and gh, with your own credentials.
  • Links you open, in your browser.

What stays on your computer

Settings, sessions, unsaved work and the local history of your files are kept in Null's own folders on your computer (~/Library/Application Support/Null, %LOCALAPPDATA%\Null or ~/.local/share/null) and nowhere else.
